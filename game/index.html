    In a game: baking and retargeting¶

    A .rigexec has no USD in it. The only thing the editor reads from the stage is each mesh's topology, UVs, material looks and touch regions, once, at load. A RigExec Character asset holds all of that next to the rig, so a packaged game loads the character with no USD and no files on disk.

    The example project uses this to make the biped playable: a third-person character whose hidden UE5 Mannequin runs Epic's locomotion animation, and a retargeter carries that animation onto the rig every frame.

    Playing the example¶

    Examples\RigExecBiped\Setup_RigExecBiped.bat     & rem once: copies the Mannequin, builds, bakes, makes the level

    Open /Game/RigExec/L_RigExecGame and press Play, or run it standalone:

    "%UE_ROOT%\Engine\Binaries\Win64\UnrealEditor.exe" Examples\RigExecBiped\RigExecBiped.uproject /Game/RigExec/L_RigExecGame -game
    Input Does
    WASD / left stick Move
    Mouse / right stick Look
    Space / gamepad A Jump
    M Show the Mannequin a metre to the side, for comparing
    - / =, 0–2 Subdivision level, live (with Squarebit Subdivs)
    Esc / gamepad B Leave the close-up; otherwise open the pause menu (in a menu: back)
    P / gamepad Start Pause menu

    The close-up¶

    A glowing circle on the floor (ARigExecCinematicPad) starts a close-up when the biped steps on it:

    1. A hard cut to a camera on the face, with letterbox bars and its own key and fill lights. The biped stops; the Mannequin's idle keeps playing.
    2. The subdivision level goes from 0 to 1 after a moment on the cage.
    3. A blink (L_UpLid/R_UpLid translated 2.735 down), then the jaw opens (M_Jaw turned 12° about X) and settles just parted. As it settles, a smile grows in: the mouth corners (L_Mouth/R_Mouth) move out and up, the upper lip (M_UpLip, L_UpLip, R_UpLip) lifts to show the teeth, and the inner brows (L_InBrow/R_InBrow) rise for a slightly surprised look.
    4. An idle that runs until Esc, looking mostly down at something in front of him. The eyes turn in the head (L_Eye/R_Eye, with M_Look's lookAt switched off) and reach each new spot in a few frames. The head and neck set off after them about 0.2 s later, the eyes ease back as it arrives, and the upper lids follow the eyes down. Blinks come every 2–5 s, sometimes doubled and often with a big look.

    The head turns ride on top of the retargeted animation through URigExecRetargetComponent::SetExtraTurn. Esc cuts back to the follow camera, sets the level to 0 and rests the face. The pad arms again once the biped has stepped off it.

    The HUD always shows SUBD LEVEL. Keys change it at any time, and in the close-up the cursor shows, so its − / + buttons can be clicked. The Subdivs component is made by class name, so the example builds without the plugin and the HUD then says so. The level is capped at 2: on the biped's 143k render vertices, level 3 takes many seconds to build.

    In the editor, Esc stops Play In Editor, so leave the close-up with gamepad B and open the menu with P, or play standalone or packaged.

    The glow material, M_RigExecGlow, is made by Scripts/setup_game.py (MakeGlowMaterial). It is an additive ring and a rippling fill.

    Packaging¶

    package_game.bat                 & rem Build\Game\Windows\RigExecBiped.exe

    CONFIG=Shipping packages a shipping build. With the editor open, set RIGEXEC_UAT_ARGS=-nocompileeditor: the editor's Live Coding blocks rebuilding the editor modules, and the game build does not need them. The stats in the menu need a Development build. The package carries no USD libraries and no .usda files: the character is the cooked RXC_Biped.

    Pause menu and settings¶

    Esc (outside the close-up), P or gamepad Start pauses the game and opens PAUSED: Resume, Choose Character (with more than one), Animation Studio (Play the Game in the studio), Settings, Quit Game. Quit closes the game (in the editor it ends Play In Editor). The menu works with the mouse, the arrow keys or W/S with Enter or Space, and the gamepad's D-pad with A and B.

    The FPS counter in the bottom-left corner shows frames per second, the frame time and the worst frame of the last second, in real time whatever the pause or time scale. Settings holds the testing switches:

    Setting Does
    FPS counter The counter on or off
    Debug stats A panel over it: game, render and GPU time, resolution, speed, subdivision level, close-up or play, the retarget's cost and error, its status
    Engine stats The engine's stat unit
    Subdivision panel The SUBD LEVEL panel on or off
    Subdivision level 0, 1 or 2 (n/a without Squarebit Subdivs)
    Show Mannequin The Mannequin a metre to the side
    Time scale ×1, ×0.5, ×0.25, ×0.1, for watching motion closely
    VSync r.VSync
    Frame cap Off, 30, 60, 120 (t.MaxFPS)
    Screen percentage 100, 75, 50 (r.ScreenPercentage), to tell a GPU limit from a CPU one

    The display settings are saved in the game's GameUserSettings.ini. Time scale, the Mannequin and the level start fresh each time. Both levels have the menu: their HUDs share ARigExecMenuHUD, which draws over each level's own HUD.

    Splash and loading screen¶

    The packaged game opens on a splash and a loading screen:

    1. Content/Splash/Splash.bmp while the engine starts (Windows draws it before the game window exists);
    2. the RigExecBipedLoadingScreen module's screen, from the moment the window opens until the level and the biped have loaded: the picture, Content/Splash/LoadingScreen.png, with a throbber and a pulsing LOADING. The movie player draws it on its own thread, so it keeps moving while the game thread loads. It shows again for any level opened later.

    The module loads before the engine starts (PreLoadingScreen), so it uses no assets: it decodes the PNG itself and hands Slate the pixels. Both files ship as loose files (DirectoriesToAlwaysStageAsNonUFS in DefaultGame.ini). It does nothing in the editor.

    The picture is a render of the biped's own face. -RigExecPortrait poses and lights it (subdivision level 2, a smile, eyes on the lens) and saves a few framings to Saved/RigExecPortrait. Scripts/make_splash.py lays one out with the title:

    Build\Game\Windows\RigExecBiped.exe /Game/RigExec/L_RigExecGame -RigExecPortrait -UseFixedTimeStep -FPS=30 -windowed -resx=1280 -resy=720
python Scripts\make_splash.py Build\Game\Windows\RigExecBiped\Saved\RigExecPortrait\portrait_0.png Examples\RigExecBiped\Content\Splash

    Test switches¶

    The character reads these from the command line, in the editor or the packaged game:

    Switch Does
    -RigExecDemo Runs a circle on its own (walk, then run), jumps every 7 s, swings the camera round, and logs the retarget's cost and error each second
    -RigExecShots With -RigExecDemo: a screenshot every 2.5 s into Saved/RigExecShots
    -RigExecQuitAfter=<s> With -RigExecDemo: quits after that many seconds (logs RIGEXEC_DEMO_DONE)
    -RigExecShowSource Starts with the Mannequin shown
    -RigExecFrames Saves every frame to Saved/RigExecFrames, for a video (with -UseFixedTimeStep -FPS=30)
    -RigExecAutoplay On the platformer level: the autopilot plays the course
    -RigExecPadDemo On L_RigExecGame: runs a lap onto the glowing pad, watches the close-up, steps the subdivision level 1 → 2 → 0 → 1, presses Esc, runs off and quits
    -RigExecPortrait On L_RigExecGame: poses and lights the face, saves four high-resolution framings to Saved/RigExecPortrait, and quits

    The platformer¶

    /Game/RigExec/L_RigExecPlatformer is a side-scrolling course with the biped as the hero. ARigExecPlatformerLevel builds it at load from an ASCII layout, which Scripts/make_platformer_layout.py generates. The course has ground with pits, bricks, ? blocks that pay out a coin when headbutted from below, pipes, staircases, coin arcs, a flagpole and a castle. Its pieces are instanced basic shapes in flat colours, with hills, bushes and clouds behind. ARigExecPlatformerCharacter is the third-person character with:

    • a side camera that leads along the course and follows the height of the ground stood on, not every jump;
    • movement locked to the course's plane;
    • a high jump that goes higher the longer Space is held.

    A/D or the left stick runs, Space jumps. The HUD shows coins, the world and the time, and COURSE CLEAR! past the flag.

    -RigExecAutoplay plays it with no one at the controls. The autopilot runs right and reads the course with traces: it jumps at a wall or step ahead, at a pit ahead, and under a ? block it hasn't hit yet. Past the flag it walks to the castle door and, when recording, quits. To record it from the packaged game:

    Build\Game\Windows\RigExecBiped.exe /Game/RigExec/L_RigExecPlatformer -RigExecAutoplay -RigExecFrames -UseFixedTimeStep -FPS=30 -windowed -resx=1280 -resy=720
ffmpeg -framerate 30 -i Build\Game\Windows\RigExecBiped\Saved\RigExecFrames\frame_%05d.png -c:v libx264 -pix_fmt yuv420p -movflags +faststart platformer.mp4

    -RigExecFrames saves every frame; with the fixed 30 fps step, each frame is 1/30 s of play.

    The animation studio¶

    A game mode of its own, ARigExecStudioGameMode, in its own level /Game/RigExec/L_RigExecStudio. Choose a character, then pose and animate it. There is no Control Rig and no editor behind it, so it ships in the packaged game.

    Ways in:

    • Tools > RigExec > Play Animation Studio in the editor opens the level and plays it. Open Animation Studio Level only opens it.
    • ANIMATION STUDIO in any level's pause menu. In the studio, the same item reads PLAY THE GAME.
    • RigExecStudioGameMode as the GameMode Override of any level, in World Settings.

    It opens on the character lobby. Character in the top bar goes back to it.

    Part Does
    TouchPose (Auto, Q, the default.) Hover the skin to light the control under it. Click to select it (Shift toggles, Ctrl removes), and drag to pose it. Across turns about the view's up, and up and down about its side. Controls that only move, or Move mode (W), follow the cursor; Ctrl swaps rotate and move for one drag, Alt twists about the view, Shift is fine. Controls no touch region reaches get a small dot to click instead
    Manipulators Move (W), Rotate (E) and Scale (R) put a manipulator on the selected control. Move has an arrow per axis its translate channels run along, and a centre square for the view's plane. Rotate has a ring per axis and an outer ring about the view. Scale has a handle per axis and a centre box for all three. Only the channels the control exposes get a handle, and Shift is fine. The skin still takes presses away from the handles; in Auto only the skin does
    Hover picker The character's picker (its baked layout), one tab per panel, drawn over the view. Clicking a button selects (Shift, Ctrl, Alt mirror). IK/FK buttons switch the limb and space buttons offer the spaces, both matched. The idle IK/FK half is drawn dim; picking it switches the limb to it, matched. [ and ] change tabs
    Channel box The selected control's translate, rotate and scale. Drag a value across to change it (Shift: fine); right-click puts it back to rest. Below: the limb's IK/FK state with a matched switch, the control's space, and its other channels
    Timeline Play and Pause (Space), step frames (← →), jump between keys (, .), Loop, Key the selection (S; nothing selected keys everything), Key All, Delete Key (Del or X), Auto Key (K, on by default: every change is keyed on the frame), the length (± 6 frames), New. The rows show every key and the selection's keys. Click a key to go to it; drag it to move it. Click or drag elsewhere to scrub
    Library Animations and poses. Click one to use it: an animation goes on the timeline, a pose is applied at the Pose mix amount and keyed when Auto Key is on. Shift-click an animation to add it at the current frame. + Save Anim and + Save Pose ask for a name. Your own items have an x to delete them; the defaults cannot be deleted
    Top bar Auto, Rotate, Move and Scale (Q, E, W, R), the Library and Picker panels, Zero (the selection, or everything), Undo and Redo (Ctrl+Z, Ctrl+Y), Frame the selection (F), Home (H), Character, Menu
    Camera Right-drag or Alt-drag orbits, middle-drag pans, the wheel zooms. Left-drag on empty space orbits, and a click there clears the selection. Esc clears the selection first, then opens the menu

    The defaults are baked on the first visit with each character. The Mannequin's Idle, Walk, Jog, Jump, Punch, Dash, Land and Fall Down go through the character's retargeter (URigExecRetargetComponent::DriveOnce) one sample a frame at 30 fps. Every third frame is kept as a key, so the clip plays like the animation and its keys stay readable on the timeline. Poses are taken along the way: Rest, Idle, Walk Contact and Passing, and so on. The clips are FK, so the IK/FK switches are keyed at FK. The bake takes a few seconds and is kept, so later visits load it. Rebake defaults bakes again. (Additive animations, such as the Mannequin's hit reacts, cannot be baked this way and are skipped.)

    Where things are kept: Saved/RigExecStudio/<Character>/. The baked defaults go in Defaults/ (with a manifest.json naming the bake version), your animations in Animations/ and your poses in Poses/. Each is a JSON file of the rig's own values, keyed by control path: a control key holds its nine transform avars, a channel key one value. Between keys, each value follows a smooth curve that never overshoots its keys; spaces and other choices hold until the next key.

    The rig runs on worker threads in the studio, as in the game, so the game thread never waits for it; while a clip plays it runs every other frame (the clip is 30 fps). A drag starts from the last pose that landed, and an IK/FK or space switch evaluates on the game thread for the pose it reads. The self-test runs it on the game thread, to read each pose straight away.

    The FPS counter (Settings > FPS counter) is drawn large on a dark plate: bottom left in the game, at the bottom left of the view in the studio. Debug stats are drawn at the same size beside it.

    -RigExecStudioTest drives every part in turn and checks the result: the bake, a clip and a pose, a rotate drag (the control turns exactly what the cursor asked, about the view's up), a move drag (the control follows the cursor), the manipulator's move arrow, view ring and scale box, keys and the curve between them, save and reload, matched IK/FK switches by button and by picking, matched space switches, TouchPose picks and a hover-picker button. It writes Saved/RigExecStudioTest/<character>.txt and screenshots, then quits:

    UnrealEditor.exe Examples\RigExecBiped\RigExecBiped.uproject /Game/RigExec/L_RigExecStudio -game -RigExecStudioTest -RigExecCharacter=Biped

    -RigExecStudioDemo, started from the game level, plays a scripted tour for a recording instead. A cursor drawn on screen clicks and drags through the real UI, with captions: the lobby, the game, the pause menu into the studio, then for each character the library, TouchPose on the face, the picker and manipulators on the body, keys and playback, matched IK/FK and space switches, and saving a pose:

    Build\Game\Windows\RigExecBiped.exe -RigExecStudioDemo -fullscreen -ResX=1920 -ResY=1080

    VR¶

    The studio plays in a PC VR headset through OpenXR: a Meta Quest over Link or Air Link, or any headset with an OpenXR runtime. The PC runs the game and the headset shows it. The project enables the OpenXR plugin. The game itself stays on the desktop, so in a headset the game level opens the studio straight away.

    To play:

    1. In the Meta Quest Link app on the PC, open Settings > General and choose Set Meta Quest Link as active next to OpenXR Runtime.
    2. Put the headset on and start Link (cable or Air Link).
    3. Run RigExecBiped_VR.bat beside the packaged game: it starts RigExecBiped.exe -vr. Or start the game as usual and turn on Settings > VR headset.

    From the editor, Play > VR Preview works the same way in L_RigExecStudio.

    You stand in front of the character. The studio's HUD is on a large panel around it, upright through the character, so the lobby, menus, library, picker, channel box and timeline are where they are on the desktop. The view in the middle is see-through. The marks the HUD draws on the character (the manipulator, TouchPose's highlight and dots) line up with it as seen from the headset. A HUD pixel maps to the world along the line from your eye through that pixel on the panel.

    Control Does
    Trigger The mouse's left button, at the laser's point on the panel: click, drag, TouchPose, manipulators, picker, timeline. Either hand; the last trigger pulled points
    Grip Holds a control: the one under the laser, or the selection. A control that moves goes where the hand goes, and turns with it if it can. A control that only turns turns as the hand turns; held within 60 cm, it swings to point at the hand and twists with the wrist. Auto Key keys it on release
    A / B Play and pause / undo
    X / Y Key the selection / frame it
    Right stick Across: a 30° snap turn about the character. Forward and back: nearer and further
    Left stick Across: a frame at a time. Forward and back: what you look at moves up and down
    Stick click Home
    Menu button The pause menu. In menus and the lobby the sticks move the highlight, A takes it, B goes back, and the trigger clicks what it points at

    The world never turns or glides under you: Home and Frame move you at once, and the right stick turns you in steps. While a headset is on, the game turns off motion blur, depth of field, lens flares, colour fringing and virtual shadow maps, and uses TAA instead of TSR (each is put back when VR is turned off). The headset's resolution comes from the Link app. Lower it there, or use Settings > Screen percentage, if the frame rate drops below the headset's.

    -RigExecVRSim runs the same VR studio on the desktop without a headset, for trying it: the camera stands where your head would be and the mouse is the right hand's laser. The left button is the trigger and the right button the grip. The self-test with it also checks the panel's mapping (a point on the character, to its panel pixel and back) and the grip (a move and a turn from afar):

    UnrealEditor.exe Examples\RigExecBiped\RigExecBiped.uproject /Game/RigExec/L_RigExecStudio -game -RigExecStudioTest -RigExecCharacter=Biped -RigExecVRSim

    The VR parts are RigExecVR.h/.cpp (ARigExecVRRig: the headset camera, the two lasers, the panel and its canvas texture) and the VR half of RigExecMenuHUD.cpp. The panel's material is /Game/RigExec/VR/M_RigExecVRPanel. It is premultiplied (alpha composite) and unlit, drawn over everything, with the camera's exposure undone so its brightness holds.

    Squarebit Eye¶

    The biped's eyes have surface projectors: each eye mesh gets the frame its iris is projected from (primvars:eyeProjector) and the eye control's pupil and iris channels packed into a matrix (primvars:eyeDials). The runtime publishes both (URigExecComponent::GetMatrixPrimvar). URigExecEyeComponent (RigExec Squarebit Eye) puts the Squarebit Eye materials on every mesh with a projector, the left and right variants by the mesh's name. Every frame after the rig evaluates, it:

    • writes the projector into the meshes' Custom Primitive Data, in the Eye's packed layout (origin and right scale, aim and pupil radius, up), on the Dynamic Mesh, the GPU skin and any Squarebit Subdivs surface;
    • sends the dials to the material: sizes multiply by 1 + dial and offsets add, as the rig's own eye shader does.

    It refers to the Eye only by asset path, so the plugin builds and runs without it, and its Status then says the materials are missing. Install it into the example with:

    install_eye.bat                  & rem copies ..\SquarebitEye\unreal\SquarebitEye into the example's Plugins

    The example's build then compiles it for this engine, and the cook takes /SquarebitEye. The character binds its eyes before it adds the subdivision surface, so the surface picks the Eye materials up.

    The character asset¶

    URigExecCharacter (Content Browser: RigExec Character) holds:

    • the .rigexec bytes;
    • the controls description, with the Control Rig element names, and the picker layout;
    • for every mesh the rig poses: face counts and indices, one UV per face corner, each face's material slot and each slot's preview-surface colour, roughness and metallic;
    • the touch regions and their colours.

    Bake one in the editor:

    unreal.RigExecRuntimeLibrary.bake_character(
    "RigExecData/Biped.rigexec", "../../../usdRig/examples/biped/Biped_stack.usda",
    "RigExecData/Biped_controls.json", "/Game/RigExec/RXC_Biped")

    Relative files are relative to the project. Setup_RigExecBiped.bat bakes RXC_Biped after it rebuilds the Control Rig, so the controls carry their element names. Bake again whenever the .rigexec or the controls change (prepare_biped.bat).

    Set Character on a URigExecComponent and it loads from the asset and ignores the file paths. The editor works the same with either: the pickers, Touch Pose and Sequencer all run from the asset.

    The retargeter¶

    URigExecRetargetComponent drives a URigExecComponent from an animated USkeletalMeshComponent. The source can be hidden; it is made to keep animating. Each frame, after the source's animation:

    1. Facing. At bind, the source is turned to face the way the rig does, from a pair of bones across the body (L_UpLeg/R_UpLeg) and a pair along it (M_Body/M_Head).
    2. Rest poses lined up. Walking down the map, each bone with an aim child is turned so it points the way its control does. This takes the Mannequin's A-pose onto the biped's T-pose, as an IK Retargeter's retarget pose does.
    3. Rotations. Each bone's world rotation relative to its lined-up rest is applied to its control's rest frame.
    4. Movement. The pelvis's movement is scaled by the two pelvis heights and carried onto M_Body.
    5. Controls. Walking the controls root first, each driven control's value is solved from the frame it should have and its parent's. The offset between them is whatever the rig last put there. That is exact for a plain hierarchy, and it keeps up with a constraint that moves it. The rig then evaluates once.

    It hands each IK/FK limb to FK first (bForceFk), and turns the component's Control Rig pull off, since the retargeter owns the controls.

    SetExtraTurn(Control, Turn) layers a turn, in the rig's asset space, on a driven control after the animation. The close-up uses it to turn the head and neck while the idle plays.

    MannequinToBiped() is the default map: pelvis, spine, neck and head, and clavicle, arm, hand, thigh, calf, foot and ball on both sides, 22 controls. Fingers are left out. The biped's finger controls hang off nulls that follow the hand through constraints, and locomotion barely moves them.

    Status says what it is driving, LastMilliseconds what a frame cost, and LastErrorDegrees the worst gap between a driven control's frame as the rig computed it and as asked: under 1° walking, up to about 5° at a run.

    Other animation¶

    Anything on the UE5 Mannequin skeleton plays as is: the example's Animation Blueprint, a montage, or Epic's Game Animation Sample. To use Fab content, add it to the project and set the character's Source Animation to its Animation Blueprint. For another skeleton, change Bones (bone, control, the aim child of each, and which one carries the movement).

    Cost¶

    On the biped, a frame takes about 15–20 ms on the game thread. Most of it is the rig evaluation and the gathering of frame publications. The retarget math is a fraction of a millisecond. Gizmo placement, about 14 ms a pose in the editor, is skipped in game worlds, because a game draws no gizmos.

    Limits¶

    • Plugin movers. A mover that usdRig plays through a USD-side library has no kernel in the Unreal runtime, and its points pass through. Today that is RigExecPatchMover (the patch curve and mouth examples). Load() warns when a rig has one, and URigExecRuntimeLibrary::RigExecInspect reports it for any file. It needs the kernel moved into a USD-free usdRig library, which the plugin would then register with SetExternalKernel.
    • FK only. Legs are driven by rotation, so feet can slide a little where the two characters' proportions differ. The Mannequin's foot IK runs on the source, before the retarget.
    • Squarebit Subdivs builds into the game when it is installed. On L_RigExecGame the character adds its Subdivs component at play (level 0 until the close-up or a key changes it); elsewhere the game draws the rig's own meshes.
    • Eyes. Without Squarebit Eye, the baked biped's eyes draw plain white: the rig's own eye shader is a glslfx for usdview.
